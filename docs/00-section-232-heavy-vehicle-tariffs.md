# Section 232 Tariffs on Heavy Vehicles: Offsets, Exemptions, and Duty Recovery for U.S. Manufacturers

---

## What Are Section 232 Tariffs on Heavy Vehicles?

Section 232 tariffs on heavy vehicles are 25% ad valorem duties imposed on all imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks (Class 3 through Class 8), truck parts, and a 10% duty on buses entering the United States. Presidential Proclamation 10984, signed in October 2025 and effective November 1, 2025, extended the existing Section 232 automotive tariff framework to cover these vehicle categories for the first time — affecting an industry that moves over 70% of the nation's freight.

## How Can U.S. Heavy Vehicle Manufacturers Reduce or Recover Section 232 Duties?

U.S. manufacturers and importers of heavy vehicles have three primary mechanisms to reduce their Section 232 exposure: the USMCA content-based partial exemption, the domestic assembly offset program, and manufacturing drawback on eligible parts. Combined, these programs can reduce the effective duty rate to zero for USMCA-qualifying vehicles and recover a meaningful portion of duties paid on parts used in U.S. assembly operations.

## What Vehicles and Parts Are Covered by the Section 232 MHDV Tariff?

The Proclamation covers a broad scope of vehicles and components classified under specific HTS headings within Chapter 87.

| HTS Heading | Description | Tariff Rate |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| 8701 | Tractors (except those principally for agricultural use) | 25% |
| 8702 | Buses, motor coaches, school buses | 10% |
| 8704 | Motor vehicles for transport of goods (Class 3–8 trucks) | 25% |
| 8705 | Special purpose motor vehicles (fire trucks, crane trucks, concrete mixers) | 25% |
| 8706 | Chassis fitted with engines for vehicles of headings 8701–8705 | 25% |
| 8709 | Works trucks (self-propelled, not fitted with lifting/handling equipment) | 25% |

Medium- and heavy-duty trucks include Class 3 to Class 8 vehicles — large pick-up trucks, moving trucks, cargo trucks, dump trucks, and tractors for eighteen-wheelers. The tariff applies to goods entered for consumption or withdrawn from warehouse on or after November 1, 2025.

As the [White House Fact Sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/10/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-addresses-the-threat-to-national-security-from-imports-of-medium-and-heavy-duty-vehicles-parts-and-buses/) stated:

> "Trucks and buses are indispensable to military readiness, military troop movements, disaster response, and the continuity of American critical infrastructure and economic stability, moving over 70% of the Nation's freight, including essential goods like food, fuel, and medical supplies."

The scope is broad. If your business imports any vehicle or component classified under these headings, you are paying the tariff — unless you qualify for one of the relief mechanisms below.

## How Does the USMCA Content Exemption Work for Heavy Vehicles?

This is the most valuable relief mechanism for manufacturers with North American supply chains. Under the Proclamation, USMCA-qualifying medium- and heavy-duty vehicles imported from Canada or Mexico can have Section 232 tariffs applied only to the non-U.S. content portion of the vehicle.

### How It Works

On February 2, 2026, the [Department of Commerce's International Trade Administration published formal procedures](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/02/2026-02049/procedures-for-submissions-by-importers-of-medium--and-heavy-duty-vehicles-qualifying-for) for importers to apply for this partial exemption. The process requires:

1. **USMCA qualification** — The vehicle must qualify for preferential tariff treatment under the USMCA
2. **U.S. content documentation** — The importer submits documentation showing the percentage of U.S.-origin content in the vehicle
3. **Commerce Department approval** — Applications are submitted electronically to MHDV232USMCAContent@trade.gov
4. **Partial tariff assessment** — Upon approval, the 25% tariff applies only to the non-U.S. content

### For USMCA-Qualifying Parts

USMCA-qualifying medium- and heavy-duty vehicle parts receive an even more favorable treatment: they are currently exempt from the Section 232 tariff entirely. Per [CBP's MHDV FAQ](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/section-232-additional-faqs-mhdv), parts that qualify for USMCA treatment and are self-certified by importers to be used for MHDV production or repair activity in the United States are classifiable under HTS 9903.74.10 — which carries a 0% Section 232 rate.

This exemption remains in effect until the Department of Commerce establishes a formal process for distinguishing U.S.- from non-U.S.-origin content within such parts.

| Scenario | Section 232 Rate |
|----------|-----------------|
| Non-USMCA heavy vehicle, any origin | 25% on full value |
| USMCA heavy vehicle (approved by Commerce) | 25% on non-U.S. content only |
| USMCA-qualifying MHDV parts for U.S. production/repair | 0% (temporary) |
| Bus from any origin | 10% on full value |

**The implication is clear:** manufacturers sourcing parts from USMCA-qualifying suppliers in Mexico or Canada can currently import those parts at zero Section 232 duty, provided they self-certify the parts are for U.S. production or repair and meet USMCA rules of origin.

## What Is the Domestic Assembly Offset Program?

The second relief mechanism is the import adjustment offset — a program designed to incentivize domestic manufacturing of heavy vehicles within the United States.

### Program Details

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Eligible entities | U.S. manufacturers of MHDVs and MHDV engines that perform final assembly in the U.S. |
| Offset amount | 3.75% of the aggregate value of all trucks assembled in the U.S. |
| What it offsets | Section 232 tariff liability incurred on imported MHDV parts |
| Program duration | November 1, 2025 through October 31, 2030 |
| Administering agency | U.S. Department of Commerce |

The 3.75% figure reflects the duty that would be owed when a 25% tariff is applied to approximately 15% of the value of a U.S.-assembled truck. In practical terms, if your company assembles heavy vehicles domestically and imports parts subject to Section 232 duties, you can apply for an annual refund that offsets a meaningful portion of those duties.

This [offset program was announced by the Department of Commerce](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/department-commerce-announces-new-auto-tariff-offset-process) and applies through 2030 — giving manufacturers a five-year window to restructure supply chains while recovering costs.

## Is Duty Drawback Available for Section 232 Heavy Vehicle Tariffs?

This is where many importers get confused — and where the details matter.

### What Is Eligible

The Proclamation allows **Direct Identification and Substitution Manufacturing Drawback** claims for eligible MHDV and passenger/light truck parts. This means that if you import parts, use them in manufacturing, and export the finished product, you may be able to recover standard customs duties paid on those parts through the drawback program.

### What Is NOT Eligible

**Section 232 duties themselves are not drawback eligible.** Per [CBP's Drawback Trade Remedies FAQ](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/news/drawback/drawback-trade-remedies-frequently-asked-questions), duties imposed under Section 232 proclamations cannot be recovered through drawback — regardless of whether the goods are subsequently exported.

Additionally, [CBP guidance](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/section-232-additional-faqs-mhdv) specifies that multiple offset or drawback claims on overlapping tariff programs will be restricted under HTSUS 9903.74, preventing double-dipping across relief mechanisms.

### What This Means in Practice

| Duty Type | Drawback Eligible? |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Standard MFN customs duty on MHDV parts | Yes (manufacturing drawback) |
| Section 232 additional duty (25%/10%) | No |
| Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF) | Yes |
| Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF) | No |

If your company manufactures heavy vehicles in the U.S. using imported parts and exports finished vehicles, you can recover the base customs duties and MPF through manufacturing drawback — but not the Section 232 surcharge. This distinction is critical for accurate duty recovery projections.

## FAQ

### What is the Section 232 tariff rate on medium- and heavy-duty vehicles?

The Section 232 tariff rate is 25% ad valorem on medium- and heavy-duty trucks (Class 3–8), truck parts, chassis, special purpose vehicles, and works trucks. Buses, including school buses and motor coaches classified under HTS 8702, are subject to a 10% tariff. These rates took effect November 1, 2025.

### Can USMCA-qualifying vehicles avoid the full 25% Section 232 tariff?

Yes. Importers of USMCA-eligible MHDVs from Canada or Mexico can apply to the Department of Commerce for approval to pay the 25% tariff only on the non-U.S. content of the vehicle. USMCA-qualifying MHDV parts used for U.S. production or repair are currently exempt from the Section 232 tariff entirely under HTS 9903.74.10.

### What is the domestic assembly offset for heavy vehicle manufacturers?

The import adjustment offset allows U.S. manufacturers who perform final assembly of heavy vehicles in the United States to claim a tariff refund equal to 3.75% of the aggregate value of all trucks they assemble domestically. The program runs from November 1, 2025 through October 31, 2030.

### Can importers claim duty drawback on Section 232 heavy vehicle tariffs?

No. Section 232 duties are not drawback eligible per CBP guidance. However, standard customs duties and Merchandise Processing Fees paid on MHDV parts that are used in manufacturing and subsequently exported can be recovered through Direct Identification or Substitution Manufacturing Drawback claims.

### Which HTS codes are covered by the Section 232 MHDV tariff?

The tariff covers vehicles and parts classified under HTS headings 8701 (tractors), 8702 (buses), 8704 (goods transport vehicles), 8705 (special purpose vehicles), 8706 (chassis with engines), and 8709 (works trucks). Specific Chapter 99 subheadings — such as 9903.74.07 and 9903.74.10 — apply depending on origin and USMCA qualification status.
